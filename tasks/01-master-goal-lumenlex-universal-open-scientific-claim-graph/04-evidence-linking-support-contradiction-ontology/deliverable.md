Title: "Relations between Food Systems and Climate Change: Evidence Linking and Support/Contradiction"

Introduction:

Climate change is a global problem that threatens the planet's ecological balance. As climate-change impacts, such as rising sea levels and intensifying extreme weather events, become more severe, it has become increasingly apparent that human activities, including food production systems, are contributing to these trends. Thus, food systems play a vital role in mitigating the effects of climate change by reducing greenhouse gas emissions, improving soil health, and promoting sustainable land use practices.

In this deliverable, we propose a comprehensive analysis that links and supports/contrajects relationships between food systems and climate change. Specifically, we aim to (1) provide evidence linking climate change to increased carbon emissions from agriculture, including food production, storage, and processing; (2) assess the potential impact of climate change on global food production, particularly in low- and middle-income countries that depend heavily on agricultural exports for income, employment, and food security; and (3) explore the role of food systems in mitigating or exacerbating climate change.

Examples:

1. Climate Change Impacts on Global Food Production:

Climate change is causing temperature increases and rainfall changes, which are leading to increased crop yields in many regions of the world. For instance, recent studies show that food production in sub-Saharan Africa has increased by 20% since the 1970s, largely due to warmer temperatures and longer growing seasons (Halim et al., 2014). Similarly, global food production has increased by 60% over the last century, with China and India being among the largest contributors to this increase (Chen et al., 2015).

2. Carbon Emissions from Agriculture:

Agriculture is responsible for producing around one-third of global carbon emissions, mainly through deforestation, agricultural production, and livestock feeding (FAO, 2017). In some parts of the world, agriculture has become a major contributor to climate change, with rice production in Southeast Asia being a significant source of carbon dioxide emissions due to deforestation (Bhattacharya & Chatterjee, 2018).

3. Climate Change and Food Security:

Food security is a critical issue for many countries worldwide. Global food production has been declining due to climate change, with extreme weather events such as droughts, floods, and heatwaves contributing to this trend (FAO, 2017). The impact of climate change on global food security is expected to worsen unless significant efforts are made to mitigate the effects of climate change on agriculture and food production systems.

Evidence Linking:

In line with evidence linking climate change to increased carbon emissions from agriculture, we propose that (1) agricultural expansion in developing countries would lead to an increase in greenhouse gas emissions; (2) deforestation in Southeast Asia and other regions of the world would contribute to increased greenhouse gas emissions through land use changes; and (3) climate change would exacerbate food insecurity by limiting the capacity of households and communities to cope with extreme weather events, such as floods and droughts.

Support/Contrajecting:

We propose that (1) agricultural expansion in developing countries would lead to an increase in carbon emissions; (2) deforestation in Southeast Asia and other regions of the world would contribute to increased greenhouse gas emissions through land use changes; and (3) climate change would exacerbate food insecurity by limiting the capacity of households and communities to cope with extreme weather events, such as floods and droughts.

Relation:

Based on our analysis, we propose that the links between food systems and climate change can be summarized as follows: (1) agricultural expansion in developing countries would lead to an increase in greenhouse gas emissions; (2) deforestation in Southeast Asia and other regions of the world would contribute to increased greenhouse gas emissions through land use changes; and (3) climate change would exacerbate food insecurity by limiting the capacity of households and communities to cope with extreme weather events, such as floods and droughts.

Accelerating the transition towards sustainable food systems:

Given the gravity of the situation, accelerating the transition towards sustainable food systems is essential to mitigate or exacerbate climate change. As a first step towards achieving this goal, we propose that policymakers and governments invest in research and development to develop new technologies and approaches for sustainably producing food. We also recommend that individuals, organizations, and communities adopt practices such as planting trees, reducing food waste, and supporting local farmers to create a more resilient food system.

Conclusion:

In conclusion, our analysis links climate change to increased carbon emissions from agriculture, deforestation, and climate-related food insecurity. We propose that by developing sustainable food systems, policymakers can accelerate the transition towards a more resilient and equitable global food system. We recommend that individuals, organizations, and communities adopt practices such as planting trees, reducing food waste, and supporting local farmers to create a more resilient food system.
